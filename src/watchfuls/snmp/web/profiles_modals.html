{# ── One word for fifteen profiles ────────────────────────────────────────── A Synology answers fifteen profiles, and assigning them one by one to every NAS in the rack is fifteen chips saying what one word says. A GROUP is an entry in the same catalogue whose members are other entries' ids instead of OIDs, so everything downstream — the field, the chips, the detection, the sampler — goes on speaking about ids. A form and not a file, unlike the profiles themselves: an OID matrix is written in an editor by somebody who knows what a walk is; a grouping is made of things that already exist, by whoever is adding the device. #}

{# ── The OID matrix, written in the panel ─────────────────────────────────── An agent answers `1.3.6.1.4.1.2021.11.9.0` with a `7` and does not say that this is the CPU or that seven is a percentage. A profile is what says so, and until now writing one meant putting a JSON file on the machine — which rules it out for the box in the rack that nobody wrote a profile for, since the person who has that box is not always the person who has a shell on the server. The form is the same document the shipped files hold, field by field, and it is validated by the same function that reads them. A metric is refused BY NAME: `normalise` drops what it cannot use and keeps the rest, which is right when reading a file somebody edited at 3am and wrong when answering a person looking at the row they just typed. #}

{# ── Choosing an OID instead of remembering one ───────────────────────────── Typing `1.3.6.1.4.1.6574.2.1.1.6` from memory is how a profile ends up measuring nothing and saying nothing about it: every digit is load-bearing, and none of them is checked by anything until a device answers — or does not. The MIBs are compiled and their symbols are already in a catalogue, so the field asks them. What a pick adds over a paste is the SYNTAX: it says whether the value is a counter (and how wide it is), a gauge, or not a number at all — the one thing about a metric nobody gets right from the OID, and the one that turns a counter wrapping around into a device that rebooted. #}
