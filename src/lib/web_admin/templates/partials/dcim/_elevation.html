{# ================================================================ The rack, drawn: front and rear, U by U, coloured by what the probes already know. This is the point of the whole section. A list of items answers "what is in this rack"; a drawing answers the question somebody actually has at three in the morning, which is **where in the cabinet do I put my hand** — and it answers it in the shape the cabinet has, so what is read on the screen matches what is seen with a torch. **Both faces, side by side.** Not a toggle: a 1U server fills U 12 from both sides, a patch panel may fill only the rear, and two half-depth devices share a U from opposite sides. A drawing that shows one face at a time makes the reader hold the other one in their head, which is the thing they came here to stop doing. **On the shared canvas** (`infra/_canvas.html`), so panning, zooming, fitting and — the one that pays for itself immediately — **exporting to PNG or SVG** are the same code the two maps use. A rack elevation is exactly the thing somebody wants to print and take downstairs. **Colour is the fleet's own.** `hostStateColor` and nothing of this file's invention: a rack and the machine list disagreeing about whether a server is in trouble would be two screens arguing in front of the person who has to decide. And the one that is not decoration: **an item with no host has no colour**. Not green. Most of what fills a rack answers to nothing, and a wall of green is precisely the lie this drawing exists to prevent. ================================================================ #} //: The id this drawing goes by on the shared canvas, and its geometry.
