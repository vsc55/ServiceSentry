{# ================================================================ Infrastructure — three more ways to read the same fleet. The list answers "which machine is in trouble" and the cards answer it from across the room. What none of them answered is the question you ask when the fleet stops being small enough to hold in your head: **which KIND of thing is in trouble** — the switches, the NAS, the hypervisors — and, once you know, which of those. Three, because they are three different readings and not three skins: * **grouped** is the table with a heading every time the group changes. It keeps the columns you chose and the order the list is sorted in, so it reads as the list you already know with the boundaries drawn in; * **rail** puts the kinds down the side and one kind on screen at a time. The grouped view shows you everything and asks you to scroll; this one shows you one thing and asks you to choose. Which of the two is better depends on how many kinds you have, which is why both exist rather than one of them being the answer; * **board** does not group by kind at all. It is the triage reading: a column per state, worst on the left, and what is in each. "What needs attention" is a different question from "what is this fleet made of", and answering it by sorting a list makes you count. WHAT IT GROUPS BY IS A CHOICE, not a fact about this file. Kind of device is the default because that is what was asked for, but a fleet tagged by rack or by customer is grouped by tag, and one being migrated is grouped by operating system.
