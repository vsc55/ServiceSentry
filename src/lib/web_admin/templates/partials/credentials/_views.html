{# ================================================================ Credentials — the part every view shares. The catalogue answers three different questions and the table only answers the first well: "what have I got" (a row per credential, sortable, with the metadata columns), "what KIND of secret is this" (SSH identities and tenant app registrations are not the same animal and the table shows them interleaved), and the one it cannot answer at all — "is anything still using this". That last one is the reason a credential catalogue rots: a secret nobody references is a secret nobody rotates, and it stays valid. So the views differ in what sits in the subject position — the credential, its type, or its consumers — and nothing else. What must NOT differ is what a credential MEANS: the type badge, the identity hint, the disabled marker and, above all, the ACTIONS a user may take are decided once (here) and composed by every view. A view that assembled its own buttons would be a view free to offer Delete to somebody who may not press it, and that is not a styling bug.
