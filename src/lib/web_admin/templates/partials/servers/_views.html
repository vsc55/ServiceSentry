{# ================================================================ Servers — what a device means, decided once: the actions, the states, the coverage. The registry's own list is gone — the fleet is listed once, in Infrastructure, which composes these — but what follows is still the one place each of them is decided. The device registry is the one list where the rows are not the point: what you actually want from it is a state of the fleet, and the table gives you that one device at a time. Three questions it leaves on the floor: * how is the fleet RIGHT NOW. There is a status column and you can sort by it, which answers "which is the worst device" and never "how many are broken". Grouped by state the answer is the first thing on screen, and an empty error group is itself the good news. * which devices are not actually being MONITORED. A device with no checks is registered and watched by nothing; a device whose checks are all disabled is worse, because somebody turned them off and the row still looks configured. Both read as ordinary rows in the table — the modules column says "0/0" and "0/3" in the same grey pill — and both mean the fleet is smaller than the list suggests. * what a device IS, as one object rather than a row of columns: its address, kind, OS, tags, clusters and check count together. That is the card view. The last two are SUMMARIES: they are handed everything the filters left standing rather than the page on screen, and they draw no pagination — a count over one page changes as you page through it, which is worse than not counting. What must NOT differ between views is what a device means: the status vocabulary, the maintenance override that wins over it, and the ACTIONS.
